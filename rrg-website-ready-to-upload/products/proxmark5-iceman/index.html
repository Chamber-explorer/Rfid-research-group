Proxmark5 Iceman Edition

Modular RFID and NFC security research hardware covering LF, HF and NFC, with UHF through an add-on module in development.

The next-generation RFID research platform from RFID Research Group. Adjustable LF from 125 to 500 kHz, HF at 13.56 MHz, dual USB-C, native Flipper Zero integration and optional WiFi and BLE wireless operation. UHF support (860 to 960 MHz) comes through an add-on module that is in development.

The firmware is community-driven and open source, and compatible with the Iceman fork.

Why we built Proxmark5

The Proxmark3 RDV4 put serious RFID research into thousands of hands. RRG watched how people used it in the field and where it ran out of room. Proxmark5 starts from those limits.

More than 30,000 RDV4 units have been deployed in about 70 countries, over the eight years since the RDV4.01.

  • LF

    Fixed low-frequency range

    Narrow LF coverage blocked custom tuning and wider experiments.

  • USB

    Tied to a cable

    Wired-only workflows slowed capture, testing and relay scenarios.

  • RF

    No wireless path

    Field work now expects WiFi and BLE for remote and flexible use.

  • CPU

    Aging architecture

    The older board design could not carry new firmware layers.

  • FPGA

    Constrained logic

    Tooling friction and tight resources made fast iteration harder than it should be.

What it does

So RRG rebuilt the board instead of revising it: a redesigned mainboard and RF routing, a newer processor and FPGA pairing, modern expansion and power paths, and room for future firmware.

Wireless operation requires the battery add-on.

Hardware architecture

Proxmark5 Iceman Edition uses a modular hardware architecture.

The main board pairs an AT32F435 processor running at 288 MHz, with 512 KB of SRAM and 1 MB of Flash, with a GOWIN GW1N-4 FPGA (4608 LUT4) and a 10-bit ADC. It has two USB-C ports. A CEP antenna interface and an XT30 port provide expansion.

Wi-Fi 4 and BLE 5 are available through the wireless battery add-on, which is sold separately. It is built around an ESP32-C2 and includes a 500 mAh, 3.7 V battery. Wireless operation requires the add-on.

Open firmware

Proxmark5 Iceman Edition runs community-driven open firmware.

The firmware is compatible with the Iceman fork of Proxmark3, which RRG hosts on GitHub.

Proxmark5 is built around the Iceman fork and an open FPGA toolchain direction. A hardware abstraction layer is on the roadmap, so the firmware can grow with new modules.

Note: Proxmark5 firmware is pre-release and under active development, so it can change between versions. Distributors give the same advice. Follow the Getting Started guide for the current build and flashing steps.
